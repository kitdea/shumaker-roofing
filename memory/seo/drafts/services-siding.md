## What Siding Work Actually Involves

Siding manages water. The panels are the visible part, but what keeps a wall dry is the layer behind them, the flashing at windows and doors, and the detailing where the wall meets the roofline. That layer is the weather-resistive barrier, a wrap that goes on before the siding and sheds whatever gets past it. The roofline joint is where wall work and roof work meet. It is also the one that leaks when the two trades never speak to each other. Our crews do both, so nobody hands off the detail and hopes.

We install fiber cement, vinyl, insulated vinyl, and engineered wood. They age differently here. Fiber cement holds paint and takes a hail strike without denting. The [Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety's hail research](https://ibhs.org/hail/) is a useful primer on what impact damage actually looks like on a wall. Vinyl costs less and never needs repainting. Insulated vinyl adds a rigid foam backer that stiffens the panel and raises the wall assembly's R-value. R-value measures how well a wall resists heat flow. Vinyl carrying the [Vinyl Siding Institute's certification](https://www.vinylsiding.org/certification/) has been checked by an independent third party against the industry standard rather than the manufacturer's own claim.

## The Step That Catches Older Homes

If the house went up before 1978, siding work that disturbs painted surfaces falls under the EPA's [Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting rule](https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-renovation-repair-and-painting-program). That rule requires the job be handled by a certified firm. It is a fair question to put to anyone bidding your work, and the time to ask is before the old siding comes off, not after.
