# Skylight Repair vs Replacement: Making the Right Call

Skylight repair vs replacement usually turns on one thing you can't see from the living room floor: the flashing. A brown ring spreading across the drywall around a skylight almost never means the glass gave out. It means water found a path around the frame and ran down the inside of the ceiling. Getting that call right is the difference between a half-day service visit and a full curb-and-flashing rebuild. This guide covers what a stain, a crack, or a foggy pane actually tells you, how the unit's age changes the math, and when a roofer should talk you out of replacing a skylight that still works.

## The Three Questions That Settle It

Before anyone writes a quote, the decision comes down to three things. A skylight crew runs the same checklist on every call.

1. **Is the glazing intact?** Glazing is the glass or acrylic panel itself, not the frame around it. A cracked or shattered pane isn't a repair. Laminated glass bonds two panes around a plastic interlayer. A hail strike hard enough to crack that means swapping the whole unit.
2. **How old is the skylight?** Residential units generally run 15 to 20 years before the seals let go. Past that, a repair buys you a season or two.
3. **Where is the water actually entering?** Flashing failures, curb failures, and seal failures each have a different fix. (The curb is the raised wooden frame the skylight sits on.) Only one of the three involves buying a new skylight.

Answer those and the decision is usually obvious. What's left is judgment about the rest of the roof. If the shingles around the opening are three years from replacement, installing a new skylight now means paying to pull the flashing apart twice.

## Most Skylight Leaks Aren't the Skylight

Water appearing at a skylight is usually a flashing problem. The unit itself is sealed, and it's built to shed water rather than hold it back. The flashing kit does the real work. It laps under the course of shingles above the skylight, over the shingles below it, and up both sides in steps.

That lap sequence is what breaks. Reusing old flashing during a roof replacement is the most common way it happens, and the drip shows up a year later looking like a failed skylight.

Condensation is the other false alarm. Moisture that forms on the inside face of the glass on a cold morning and dries off by noon is room humidity, not a leak. An attic without balanced intake and exhaust makes it worse, which is a [ventilation problem rather than a skylight problem](/blog/why-attic-ventilation-matters-in-summer-frederick-md).

Either way, speed matters once water is inside. The EPA's guidance on [moisture and mold in the home](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) puts the window at 24 to 48 hours to dry water-damaged materials before mold takes hold. A ceiling stain that's been there a month has stopped being only a roofing conversation.

## Repair Territory vs Replacement Territory

### What a repair handles

Flashing that has lifted, split, or was never lapped correctly. A worn gasket letting wind-driven rain past the frame. A shade motor that quit. Haze on the exterior glass that cleans off. Every one of these leaves the skylight in the roof. A ten-year-old unit with a bad flashing kit is a service call. A crew that quotes you a replacement for it is selling rather than diagnosing.

### What forces a replacement

A cracked pane, which can't be patched back to watertight. Fog trapped between the panes, which means the seal has failed and the insulating gas is gone. Rotted decking or curb framing around the opening. Any unit past 20 years that's already leaking. And the practical case: when a [full roof replacement](/services/roof-replacement) is happening anyway, the skylight goes with it, because the flashing has to be new regardless.

The warranty terms are worth reading before you choose a unit. VELUX, whose skylights our crews install, [publishes](https://www.veluxusa.com/warranty) 20 years on the glass against seal failure, 10 years on the product, and 10 years on the installation. Notice which term is shortest. The workmanship carries less coverage than the glass does.

## What Actually Moves the Price

There's no flat rate for this work, and anyone who quotes a skylight over the phone is guessing at the flashing. Four things do most of the moving:

- **Curb-mounted or deck-mounted.** Deck-mounted units sit lower and use a different flashing kit. Swapping between the two changes the labor.
- **Whether the opening needs framing work.** Rot in the curb or decking turns a replacement into carpentry.
- **The glass spec.** ENERGY STAR certified skylights have to meet [U-factor and solar heat gain limits set by climate zone](https://www.energystar.gov/products/res_windows_doors_skylights). U-factor measures how much heat escapes through the glass. Solar heat gain measures how much sun heat comes through it. Maryland's limits aren't Florida's, so the glass that qualifies here is different.
- **Access and pitch.** A steep roof or a second-story vault means staging, and staging is time.

Once the flashing is priced in, a repair and a replacement can land closer together than homeowners expect. That gap is why an on-site look beats a phone estimate. Our team will tell you when the repair is the better buy.

## What the Mid-Atlantic Does to Skylights

Freeze-thaw is the local villain. Meltwater runs down a sun-warmed slope, refreezes at the cold eave, and backs up under the flashing on the north side of the roof. That's the same mechanism behind [winter ice dam damage](/blog/three-common-winter-roof-damage-issues-and-how-to-prevent-them). Spring hail is the second one, and it cracks glazing on older acrylic domes long before it bothers laminated glass. Then July humidity arrives and every marginal seal starts fogging.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a skylight last?

Plan on 15 to 20 years for a residential unit, with the seal usually going before anything else does. Sun exposure drives most of the variation, so a south-facing skylight ages faster than a north-facing one on the same house. Installation quality matters as much as the product does. VELUX backs its glass against seal failure for 20 years and its installations for 10, which is a fair guide to where the risk sits.

### Can a cracked skylight be repaired?

No. A crack has already broken the pane's ability to keep water out and hold its insulating value. Sealant laid over the top of it fails within a season. Cracked units get replaced. If yours cracked after a storm, photograph it before anything else happens, since your insurer will want dated evidence of the damage.

### Why does my skylight only leak in heavy rain or in winter?

Both patterns point at flashing rather than the skylight. Wind-driven rain gets pushed sideways under a lap that would shed a vertical downpour without trouble. Winter leaks usually mean ice is backing water up the slope. A unit that stays dry in ordinary rain and drips in these two conditions is telling you the water is getting around it, not through it.

### Should I replace the skylight when I replace the roof?

Almost always, if the unit is more than about ten years old. The flashing has to be replaced with the shingles either way, and that's most of the labor. Putting a 15-year-old skylight back into a brand-new roof means opening the same section again in a few years. Our estimates price the skylight both ways so you can see the difference.
