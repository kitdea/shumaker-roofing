# Banned AI Words

Reference file. The workflow lives in the seo-writer skill (Step 4 and the Step 5 self-check); this file is the ban lists, carve-outs, and active-language reference that step calls. Scan every sentence against it.

Two principles first:
- Tier deliberately. HARD BAN is zero tolerance in your own prose. EARN IT is allowed only when literally accurate and no plainer word fits. Zero-tolerance applied to common English produces contortions that read as "off" in their own way.
- The deepest tell is genericness, not vocabulary. A page built from approved plain words that says nothing net-new still reads as machine-made. This list is the floor. Information gain is the ceiling. Specificity (numbers, names, local detail, prices, dates) is the real antidote.

## How to apply the bans (carve-outs, so you do not contort)

- Quotes and proper nouns are exempt. Do not edit a real customer quote, a brand name, a product name, a study title, or a citation to dodge a banned word. Altering a quoted source also breaks reference integrity.
- Literal accuracy wins. If a banned word is the precise technical term (for example "robust" in an engineering spec the client supplied, or "navigate" for literal directions), it is allowed. The ban targets figurative and decorative use.
- Target the sentence shape, not just the word. Swapping vocabulary while keeping the banned structures still reads as AI. Fix the rhythm.
- Do not produce contortions. If avoiding a word forces awkward phrasing, rewrite the whole sentence plainly rather than reaching for a thesaurus synonym.

## Banned sentence structures (these survive word-swaps; rewrite or cut)

- Participial benefit-closer (the -ing tail): "..., saving you time and money," "..., ensuring a smooth process," "..., allowing you to focus on what matters." Rewrite as a separate direct sentence or cut.
- Not only X, but also Y.
- It's not just X, it's Y. / That's not X, that's Y. / It's not about X, it's about Y.
- Not because X, but because Y. / Not by doing X, but by doing Y.
- No X. No Y. Just Z.
- X. Y. Z. (three one-word or two-word fragments, e.g. "Focused. Aligned. Measurable.")
- And the X? Y. / The result? Y. (rhetorical question then a one or two word answer)
- Whether you're a [A] or a [B].
- From [X] to [Y] (false range used to fake comprehensiveness).
- more than just [X].
- Despite [challenge], [subject] [verb] (the struggle-arc framing).
- Staccato triad: "Fast. Affordable. Done." Vary to two or four, or use a full sentence.
- Pseudo-conversational openers: "Here's the thing:", "Here's the deal:", "The best part?", "Spoiler:", "Plot twist:", "Here's why that matters:".
- Spaced-period emphasis: "This. Changes. Everything."
- Rhetorical-question openers and the symmetrical intro-body-conclusion shape.
- Listicles where every bullet opens with the same verb or structure.
- Generic analogies: "Think of X like Y."

## Model tics, formatting, and punctuation bans

Assistant tics (never appear in published copy): Certainly!, Sure! Here's, Absolutely!, Of course!, Great question!, I hope this helps!, Let me know if you need any modifications, As an AI language model, As of my last update, Here's a breakdown, straightforward, that said, here's the thing, it's worth considering, one could argue, overarching, underpinning, in essence.

Formatting and punctuation:
- No em dashes or en dashes anywhere (the strongest single fingerprint). Use commas, periods, parentheses, or hyphens.
- No mid-sentence bold for emphasis and no bold scattered through prose.
- No emoji in headers, inline, or as bullets. No arrow glyphs as connectors.
- No bullet overuse where prose belongs; no colon-then-dump-into-a-list reflex; no numbered-list reflex when a sentence fits.
- No bolt-on "Key Takeaways" or "TL;DR" boxes unless the client asks.
- No uniform paragraph length; no "heading then exactly three bullets" repeated mechanically.
- No leaked markdown (double-asterisk bold, hash headings) when delivering to a CMS field.
- No curly smart quotes in plain-text fields. Sentence case headings, not Title Case On Every Heading.

## Active language (do this instead)

- Use contractions consistently (don't, you'll, it's).
- Use colloquial language where it fits. "That's a rip-off." "Nobody's falling for that."
- Vary transitions; sometimes use none. Just start the next thought.
- Start some sentences with And or But when it fits, not at fixed intervals.
- Use occasional parenthetical asides (like this) for a human beat.
- Vary sentence length on purpose. A three-word sentence next to a 30-word one creates rhythm; uniform length reads as machine.
- Lead every section with a standalone, liftable answer (for AI citation), then prove it with specifics.
- Replace adjectives with data. "$40 a month," not "affordable." "installed in 3 days," not "fast."
- Name one real thing per paragraph: a price, a date, a street, a brand, a number.
- Take a position. One idea per sentence. Confident, not hedged.
- Read it aloud. If you would not say it to a customer's face, cut it.

## Vertical pack: home remodeling / local services

The fastest tell here is interchangeability: a sentence a competitor could paste verbatim is neither differentiated nor de-AI'd. Replace every banned item with the thing only this business can say:
- The actual permit process and timeline in that city.
- Real lead time and project duration in weeks.
- A named past project on a named street or neighborhood.
- The exact cabinet, fixture, or material brands installed.
- Specific warranty terms (years, what is covered).
- What goes wrong in that area's older housing stock and how it is handled.
- Real price ranges or starting points.

Alt text in this vertical: plain and specific ("white shaker kitchen remodel, [city], quartz counters"), not "a beautiful modern kitchen featuring state-of-the-art appliances."

## Ban-specific self-check (in addition to the seo-writer Step 5 self-check)

- No em or en dashes.
- No HARD BAN words; EARN IT words only where literally accurate; carve-out for quotes and proper names respected.
- No banned phrases, sentence structures, or model tics.
- No participial -ing benefit-closers.
- No authority superlatives or unverifiable claims (best, #1, premier, unmatched).
- Sentence length and openers vary; contractions used; reads aloud naturally.
- Interchangeability test passed: no sentence a competitor could paste verbatim.
